## Problem

An accepted inbound QUIC connection parses the remote peer's certificate three times and performs seven signature verifications. One parse and three verifications carry all the information; the rest is repetition, paid in server CPU on every connection of every swarm, by honest validators as much as by strangers — remotes in no committee the node tracks and on no operator allowlist, which the node admits today. What share of a handshake it costs has not been measured on the crates now resolved.

`libp2p_tls::certificate::parse` is a DER parse plus two signature verifications:

```rust
let certificate = parse_unverified(certificate.as_ref())?;
certificate.verify()?;
```

(`libp2p-tls-0.7.0/src/certificate.rs:127-129`.) `P2pCertificate::verify` checks the certificate's self-signature (`certificate.rs:398`) and the libp2p extension's host-key signature (`:406-408`, `:414-417`). What the first of those two costs is chosen by the remote, not by the node: `signature_scheme` reads the scheme out of the certificate presented (`certificate.rs:428-517`), and every scheme it admits but ECDSA P-521 and Ed448 — RSA and ECDSA P-384 included — reaches a `ring` algorithm and verifies (`:334-347`, `:340-343`, `:348-351`). `libp2p-tls` generates ECDSA P-256 certificates (`certificate.rs:45`) for Telcoin Network's Ed25519 identities (`crates/config/src/keys.rs:553`), so the repetition multiplies a per-verification cost the presenter partly chooses.

The server reaches it three times per full handshake — one that is not resumed. `verify_client_cert` calls `verify_presented_certs`, which parses and returns a `PeerId` the caller discards (`libp2p-tls-0.7.0/src/verifier.rs:178`, `:222`): verifications 1 and 2. `verify_tls13_signature` parses the same bytes again before checking the transcript signature, the `CertificateVerify` message proving the far end holds the certificate key (`verifier.rs:233`, called from `:192-198`): verifications 3, 4 and 5. The transport then parses a third time for the `PeerId` (`libp2p-quic-0.14.0/src/connection/connecting.rs:75`, called from `:90`): verifications 6 and 7. The count is exact: `rustls` drives each callback once, from two one-shot states (`rustls-0.23.37/src/server/tls13.rs:1081-1083`, `:1133-1135`), and `libp2p-tls` allows only TLS 1.3 (`verifier.rs:48`).

## Goal

- **The repetition's share of a full inbound handshake is known for the resolved crates, and so is how far the presenter's choice of certificate moves it.** *Observable:* server CPU per full inbound handshake, split by where it is spent, from **the cost split** below, read for a committee peer's certificate and again for one whose signature scheme the presenter chose. *Baseline:* the cost split sets it; the nearest figures today are the ones under Measurements, which predate the `aws_lc_rs` provider (`libp2p-tls-0.7.0/src/lib.rs:48`, `:76`) and are not reproduced on the resolved crates.
- **If the share the cost split prices justifies it, a full inbound handshake repeats less certificate work.** *Observable:* per-handshake counts of certificate parses and of signature verifications, separating the work that establishes something — the parse that yields the `PeerId`, and the three signature checks (see Background) — from the work that repeats it. *Baseline:* three parses and seven signature verifications on `main` at `9b2a06b7`, of which one parse and three verifications carry all the information; **the cost split** below prices each repeat and says what reading those counts depends on, and the maintainers set from it which repeats, if any, are worth removing. A resumed handshake pays one parse and two verifications.
- **Honest peers unaffected.** Every peer that connects today still connects and resolves to the same `PeerId`, and a wrong self-signature, extension signature or `CertificateVerify` signature still fails. *Observable:* the committee end-to-end tests, a test tampering with each of those three signatures and asserting refusal, and one handshake from a dialer on stock `libp2p-quic` 0.13.x and `libp2p-tls` 0.6.2.
- **Non-goals.** Where a refusal happens ({{late-refusal-of-strangers}}). Which key-exchange group the server computes ({{dialer-chosen-kx-group}}). How many handshakes are accepted ({{validated-address-handshake-rate}}), and how a swarm accounts for inbound connections whose handshake has not finished ({{pending-inbound-unbounded}}).
- **Closed by** the cost split and, if the share it prices justifies the work of removing a repeat (including the upkeep of a modified crate, where the approach taken needs one: {{patched-transport-crate-upkeep}}), a lower parse and verification count read the same way, with the honest-peer observables passing. If the share is small, the cost split closes the issue on its own.

## Background

**Threat model.** The attacker is outside the validator set and is assumed to know every validator's IP address, port, `PeerId` and BLS public key. They can forge source addresses, and they can rent a botnet of hosts with real addresses. They hold no committee key and cannot break cryptography.

**Acceptance rules.** A defence has to hold when the host firewall is absent or wrong, and has to fail open when an input it depends on (a committee list, an address list) is stale or missing, so honest peers keep connecting. Floods that saturate the link are the hosting provider's to absorb and are out of scope; the scope is what such an attacker can do to the node's CPU, memory and tasks at packet rates the NIC still delivers.

**Node shape.** Each libp2p swarm has one public QUIC listener on one UDP socket. A node runs one swarm for the primary and one per worker (`crates/node/src/manager/node.rs:1244`, `:1282`), all built by `ConsensusNetwork::new` (`crates/network-libp2p/src/consensus.rs:406`), so every per-swarm limit or cost multiplies by the number of swarms on the host.

Paths under `crates/` refer to `main` at `9b2a06b7`; a path that begins with a crate name and version, such as `libp2p-quic-0.14.0/src/transport.rs:595`, refers to the crates.io source of the version `Cargo.lock` resolves.

**Reference deployment.** The maintainers' reference deployment is a committee of ten validators, each on an Ubuntu server with 8 CPUs and 32 GB of RAM, running one node process with two swarms (the primary's and one worker's). The network between validators (link speed and round-trip times) has not been specified.

Three signatures stand between a presented certificate and an authenticated peer. `P2pCertificate::verify` checks the certificate's self-signature over its own contents (`certificate.rs:398`), then the libp2p extension's signature, made with the peer's host key over the public key the certificate carries, which is what proves the holder of that host key issued this certificate (`certificate.rs:401-417`). The third is the `CertificateVerify` message, checked in the other verifier callback (`verifier.rs:233`): it proves the far end holds the certificate's private key in this handshake, rather than replaying a certificate copied from someone else. No two of the three establish the same thing.

A node dialling out pays the same sequence against the server's certificate. `verify_server_cert` parses and verifies at `verifier.rs:116`, then compares the `PeerId` it returns with the peer the dial named, where it named one (`:118-127`); `verify_tls13_signature` parses the same bytes again at `:148`; and the transport's parse runs whichever side opened the connection, since a dial and an accept both go through `Connecting` (`libp2p-quic-0.14.0/src/transport.rs:308`, `:610`). Dialling a peer therefore costs the dialler what accepting one costs the listener.

A resumed handshake carries no client certificate, so neither callback runs (`rustls-0.23.37/src/server/tls13.rs:340`, `:380`; the chain is restored at `:333-338`) and only `libp2p-quic`'s parse remains. Resumption is live at the `rustls` defaults `make_server_config` leaves alone (`libp2p-tls-0.7.0/src/lib.rs:71-93`; `server/builder.rs:110`, `:120`), but likely rare: `rustls` keys stored client sessions on the server name (`client/handy.rs:73-74`) while `libp2p-quic` dials every peer under one name, `"l"` (`libp2p-quic-0.14.0/src/transport.rs:302-306`), so one entry serves every remote. How much honest traffic resumes is unmeasured.

### Approaches surveyed so far

- Have `libp2p-quic` take the `PeerId` from the completed handshake instead of re-parsing. quinn hands over the certificate chain, not the verifier's output (`connecting.rs:66-71`), and the TLS configurations are private fields set only in `Config::new` (`libp2p-quic-0.14.0/src/config.rs:66-68`, `:80-85`). It runs in a modified copy of `libp2p-quic` until an upstream release carries it ({{patched-transport-crate-upkeep}}).
- Let a caller read the `PeerId` without verifying. `certificate::parse` is public API documented to guarantee the signature matches the embedded key (`certificate.rs:120-123`), `parse_unverified` is private (`:170`), and `peer_id` and `verify_signature` are public on the parsed value (`:299`, `:305`). Changing what that crate offers a caller runs in a modified copy of `libp2p-tls` for as long as it runs ahead of an upstream release ({{patched-transport-crate-upkeep}}).
- Parse and verify once in the verifier, then carry the result to the second callback. `rustls` drives the two callbacks from separate one-shot states (`rustls-0.23.37/src/server/tls13.rs:1016`, `:1104`) sharing no per-handshake storage, and one verifier object serves every concurrent handshake on the swarm (`libp2p-tls-0.7.0/src/lib.rs:87`). Storage bridging the two callbacks runs in a modified copy of `libp2p-tls` until a release carries it ({{patched-transport-crate-upkeep}}).
- Upstream it. The duplication spans two crates in `libp2p/rust-libp2p`, each on its own release cadence, and a search of that repository's issues and pull requests for duplicate certificate parsing and verification caching returned nothing, so it would begin there as a proposal.

## Implementation details to pay close attention to

- Both callbacks are `&self` methods on the one verifier object the server config shares across concurrent handshakes (`libp2p-tls-0.7.0/src/verifier.rs:163`, `:172`, `:192`; see Approaches), so anything carried from the first callback to the second is carried in storage every handshake can reach. If a verification result held there outlives the handshake that produced it, its key and bound are attacker-controlled — a `PeerId` and a certificate are free to generate — and a certificate that verified before says nothing about who presents it now: one host key signs both certificates a peer generates (`libp2p-quic-0.14.0/src/config.rs:80-85`), and only the `CertificateVerify` check (`verifier.rs:233`) binds one to this handshake.
- If a parse that skips verification becomes reachable, `P2pCertificate::peer_id` (`certificate.rs:299`) returns a `PeerId` from an unchecked extension, and every caller treating a `PeerId` as authenticated inherits that.
- `PeerId` extraction has to succeed before the swarm sees the connection (`connecting.rs:90`), so if it can fail where today it cannot, a peer that completed the handshake is dropped unexplained.
- If anything a remote observes changed — the certificate presented, or the alert sent on failure — a peer on stock `libp2p-quic` 0.13.x and `libp2p-tls` 0.6.2 would see a failure indistinguishable from a bad certificate. The duplication is local to each side of a handshake.
- An honest mass reconnect — a restart, a partition healing, an epoch committee change — pays this on every peer of every swarm at once: on the reference deployment, nine remote committee members per swarm and two swarms in the process. The count per handshake is fixed; how many handshakes arrive is the subject of {{validated-address-handshake-rate}}, and how a swarm accounts for the ones whose handshake has not finished is the subject of {{pending-inbound-unbounded}}.

## Measurements that would affect the decision

Every figure below was taken in process on one desktop-class Intel CPU, and the absolute microseconds run high because CPU frequency could not be pinned; ratios within a run are unaffected. One parse of a libp2p certificate carrying an Ed25519 identity — a DER parse plus the two signature verifications it performs — took 70.6 µs at the median with `libp2p-tls` 0.6.2; only the Ed25519 identity case was measured, and it is not reproduced on current versions. A full inbound QUIC and TLS handshake from an unknown peer, one holding no session ticket, cost the server 285.8 µs of CPU at the median and 319 µs at the 99th percentile, driving the real libp2p TLS configurations with `libp2p-tls` 0.6.2 and `rustls` 0.23.37 on the ring crypto provider; that total is `rustls`'s work alone, two parses and about five verifications, excluding `libp2p-quic`'s third parse, so it understates the repetition's share, and it is not reproduced on current versions. A resumed handshake on the same driver — the PSK binder and key schedule, no certificate flight — cost the server 86.0 µs at the median; only a returning peer that already holds a session ticket reaches that path, so the figure bounds no stranger's cost, and it is not reproduced on current versions. Of the primitives a parse runs, one ECDSA P-256 signature verification took 40.0 µs at the median through `ring` 0.17.14, and one Ed25519 verification took 25.6 µs through `ed25519-dalek` 2.2.0, the implementation that checks the host-key signature carried in a libp2p certificate; both are microbenchmarks of 100 samples on the same host, and neither is reproduced on current versions.

The move to `aws_lc_rs` supersedes less of this than it appears to. `libp2p-tls` 0.7.0 builds both `rustls` configurations on that provider (`libp2p-tls-0.7.0/src/lib.rs:48`, `:76`) where 0.6.2 built them on ring (`libp2p-tls-0.6.2/src/lib.rs:48`, `:75`), takes its three TLS 1.3 cipher suites from it (`verifier.rs:33-35`, against `libp2p-tls-0.6.2/src/verifier.rs:31`), and signs the server's own `CertificateVerify` with a key it produces (`certificate.rs:57`), so the provider governs the key exchange, the cipher suite and that signature — the parts of the 285.8 µs that are not the repeated certificate work. The repeated work itself does not run on it. `P2pCertificate::public_key` hands back a `ring::signature::UnparsedPublicKey` (`certificate.rs:318-324`, constructed at `:364`), and the extension signature goes through `libp2p_identity::PublicKey::verify`, which for an Ed25519 identity is `ed25519-dalek` (`libp2p-identity-0.3.0/src/ed25519.rs:143`); 0.6.2 took both of those routes as well (`libp2p-tls-0.6.2/src/certificate.rs:303-304`, `:389-392`). What supersedes the figures for that work is instead `ed25519-dalek` 2.2.0 to 3.0.0 and `libp2p-identity` 0.2.13 to 0.3.0, which `Cargo.lock` resolves through `libp2p-tls` 0.7.0, and `x509-parser` 0.17.0 to 0.18.1 under the DER parse; `ring` is 0.17.14 in both, and `rustls` 0.23.37 in both.

What one honest reconnect costs a host on the reference deployment is arithmetic, derived and not measured. Nine remote committee members reach each swarm (a committee of ten, see Background) and the process runs two swarms, so nine remote hosts reach two swarms each: eighteen peer-to-swarm pairs on one host. Today's code lets each pair hold up to eight concurrent established connections across both directions (`MAX_ESTABLISHED_CONNECTIONS_PER_PEER` is 8 at `crates/network-libp2p/src/consensus.rs:124`, installed at `:133-138`), which permits 144 per host. The comment on that constant records honest use as one inbound and one outbound connection per peer (`:118-122`), so an honest mass reconnect is eighteen inbound handshakes per host. At the 285.8 µs median above that is about 5.1 ms of server CPU for the honest shape, and about 41 ms in total to establish 144, the ceiling that cap permits were every one of them inbound; the repetition is the share of it **the cost split** measures. Both products inherit the limits of the figure they multiply: superseded crates, a desktop-class host that is not the reference host, CPU frequency not pinned, and `libp2p-quic`'s third parse excluded.

**The cost split.** On the crates `Cargo.lock` resolves, measure server CPU per full inbound handshake and split it into the time inside `libp2p_tls::certificate::parse` and the rest. That split prices a parse; the per-handshake call counts then say how much of the total is repetition. Alongside the CPU figure the run has to count how many times the certificate is parsed and how many signature verifications execute, telling the parse that yields the `PeerId` and the three signature checks apart from the two parses and four verifications that establish nothing those have not already established. Every one of those calls happens inside `libp2p-tls` and `libp2p-quic` (see Problem), so the counts do not come out of node-owned code as the crates stand, and reading them depends on a modified copy of each ({{patched-transport-crate-upkeep}}). Run it on the reference host (see Background). The key types a committee peer presents are not a variable (see Problem), while a remote outside the committee presents what it likes, so a second reading with an RSA or ECDSA P-384 certificate measures the range the presenter controls. The honest reconnect rate is still an input the maintainers supply, and it turns on the network the reference deployment leaves unspecified. If the repetition is a large enough share at that rate, it is worth acting on; if it is small, the issue closes on the measurement. A build with some of the repeat calls elided — which calls, and how, follows from whichever approach is taken — would confirm the share after that decision rather than produce it, and it needs the same modified copies the counts already do.

**The resumed share.** A resumed handshake pays one parse and two verifications, so the fraction of honest inbound handshakes that resume scales everything above; count the two classes on a node carrying committee traffic. What has to be told apart is whether a handshake ran the certificate flight at all, which is the kind of handshake `rustls` performed: it records that on the connection state and exposes it (`rustls-0.23.37/src/common_state.rs:188`), while the handshake data `quinn` hands out of the TLS session carries the negotiated protocol and the server name, not the kind (`quinn-proto-0.11.18/src/crypto/rustls.rs:259-267`, built at `:60-68`), so this count depends on a modified crate as the cost split's counts do ({{patched-transport-crate-upkeep}}). If nearly all honest inbound handshakes are full ones, the figures above apply to nearly all of them; if a large fraction resume, what remains of the repetition falls mostly on handshakes from peers holding no session ticket — every first connection, and every remote whose stored entry is not a ticket this node issued — so removing it saves less on steady-state committee traffic than those figures suggest. If the two classes cannot be told apart on a node under real traffic, the fraction stays unknown and the cost split has to be read as applying to every inbound handshake.

## Open questions

- **Which issue owns the presenter's choice of certificate signature scheme?** The Problem records that the remote, not the node, picks the scheme the first of the two verifications inside a parse runs (`certificate.rs:428-517`), and the first Goal item has the cost split price the range that choice covers; what to do about the choice itself is another matter, since removing repetition does not change what one verification costs. What would settle it: a decision by the maintainers on whether it sits with {{dialer-chosen-kx-group}}, which owns the other per-handshake cost the far end picks, or in an issue of its own.
- **Would `rust-libp2p` take a change to this behaviour, and on what timeline?** The duplication sits in two of its crates, each on its own release cadence, and a search of that repository turned up nothing on it (see Approaches). The answer sets what the upstream route costs in calendar time and how long a modified copy of either crate would run ahead of a release ({{patched-transport-crate-upkeep}}), so it bears on every surveyed approach. What would settle it: raising it in that repository, or a decision by the maintainers on who does.
- **What do the figures above come to on the crates `Cargo.lock` resolves?** Each is marked not reproduced on current versions, and until the cost split runs they are the nearest thing the first Goal item has. **The cost split** re-establishes the handshake total and the split inside it but not the per-primitive costs, so re-running the parse and the two verification microbenchmarks on `ed25519-dalek` 3.0.0 and `libp2p-identity` 0.3.0 is a follow-up of its own.
- **At what honest reconnect rate should the cost split be read?** Its decision rule turns on the repetition's share at that rate, and the arithmetic above gives a cost per reconnect rather than a cost per second; the reference deployment does not specify the network between validators, which is what sets how often a committee reconnects and how fast the burst arrives. What would settle it: that input from the maintainers, alongside **the resumed share**, which says how much of the traffic pays the full cost at all.
- **Where does the mixed-version handshake in the honest-peer observable run?** The third Goal item asks for one handshake from a dialer on stock `libp2p-quic` 0.13.x and `libp2p-tls` 0.6.2, and the workspace declares a single `libp2p` version (`Cargo.toml:273`), so no test here builds that dialer today. What would settle it: {{quic-listener-interop-tests}}, which owns whether the suite gains coverage of that kind.
